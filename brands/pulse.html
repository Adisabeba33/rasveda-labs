PULSE

The next-generation walkie-talkie

Your people.
One frequency.

Hold the button and your people hear you — across the street or across the country. PULSE brings the immediacy of a walkie-talkie to the internet, with a pager, a team map and live radio in the same app.

The idea

A shared frequency.
A reason to be there.

PULSE starts with a simple social gesture: agree on a frequency and a code, or send an invitation link. Everyone tuned to the same pair shares the air. Hold the talk key, speak, and release it for the next person.

That makes it useful for friends, a team out together or a community gathering. When people are away, messages wait on the pager. When you want to discover a conversation, the public dial brings together open waves and radio stations.

The experience

One radio. Four ways to stay close.

Live voice

Talk on your frequency

Push-to-talk voice between devices, a shared frequency and access code, saved presets and invitations by link or QR. The server gives one person the floor at a time and queues the next speaker.

The pager

Leave something for later

Group and personal messages, voice history, photos and files keep the conversation going between live transmissions. The radio’s display can surface incoming messages and let you reply.

Team map

Know where to meet

Share your position on the channel, drop a meeting point or send an SOS marker to the people tuned in. Walkie-talkie maps require a six-digit access code; live positions stay in server memory.

Public air

Find a wave. Start your own.

Browse open conversations and radio stations, enter a conversation as a listener and request the floor. Create a wave, upload station tracks or use the studio to present a live broadcast.

Product & design

The phone becomes
an instrument.

A readable frequency display, a dedicated talk key, tactile controls and restrained signal light make the radio the centre of the experience. Graphite, titanium and mint are the default identity; a collection of skins gives the same controls different physical characters.

The pager lives inside the radio display. Public waves, messages and the profile carry the selected skin through the app, so moving between them still feels like using one device.

Optional video adds the current speaker’s face to a transmission on supported browsers. The camera is opt-in, video uses the same six-digit-code requirement as the map, and voice takes priority when the connection is busy.

Under the hood

Built around the right to speak.

A server-controlled floor

A Go server arbitrates each channel. Transmission grants expire unless renewed; a queue, talk limits and disconnect handling keep a lost connection or stuck button from holding the air.

A browser audio pipeline

The JavaScript client captures and plays voice through Web Audio and AudioWorklets. Persistent WebSocket connections carry events and media, while an installable PWA provides a home-screen entry point.

Persistence & private messages

PostgreSQL persistence and Supabase Storage support messages and media. Personal-chat text and files use WebCrypto end-to-end encryption. Live voice is relayed by the server; encryption at rest is a separate deployment setting.

Where it stands

Working on the web.
Still being refined.

The repository contains a working Go server and web client with push-to-talk, messaging, public waves, radio, team maps and optional speaker video. The current delivery is a web app that can be added to the home screen.

Mobile audio routing, background behaviour and video support depend on the browser and device and remain areas for testing. Native applications are a further development track. PULSE uses an internet connection; the frequency is the shared address of a conversation.
